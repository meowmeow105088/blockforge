## BLOCKFORGE — The Legend of the Seven Worlds
Seven worlds. Seven ancient powers. One shattered reality.
## The Seven Worlds
A journey across seven dimensions to uncover the truth behind the shattered universe.
1. The Seed — Verdantia
World of Life
A vast overgrown world where nature remembers the ancient past and refuses to die.
2. The Ember — Pyroclast
World of Creation
A volcanic realm of legendary smiths, living metal, and weapons forged in impossible heat.
3. The Frost — Nivara
World of Time
A frozen kingdom trapped in repeating moments, where the past never stays buried.
4. The Sun — Solara
World of Light
A shining celestial realm where every shadow conceals a secret and truth can be dangerous.
5. The Void — Umbryss
World of Space
A reality-bending dimension of portals, impossible landscapes, and worlds that never existed.
6. The Storm — Voltrix
World of Energy
An industrial world of enormous machines, electrical storms, and a civilization that vanished without warning.
7. The Origin — Axiom
World of Reality
The oldest and most mysterious world, where the laws of creation can be rewritten.
## PROLOGUE — The First Spark
Before the stars, before time, and before the first block was ever created, there was only the Null.
The Null was an endless emptiness where nothing existed. There were no worlds, no creatures, no light, and no darkness. There was not even time for the emptiness to pass.
Then, in the middle of nothing, a spark appeared.
Nobody knows where it came from.
Some ancient records claim it was born from a thought. Others claim it was the first thing to escape the Null. The oldest surviving inscription gives it a different name:
The First Beginning.
The spark grew brighter until its light spread across the emptiness. From that light came matter, and from matter came the first block.
The block floated in the endless void, perfectly still.
Then another block appeared.
And another.
Soon, an enormous structure began to form around the spark. Stone pillars rose from nothing, rivers of molten light flowed through empty space, and enormous machines began turning without anyone to operate them.
The structure became known as the First Forge.
The First Forge possessed the ability to create anything. A single block could become a mountain. A handful of materials could become an entire civilization. Even the laws that governed reality could be changed by those who understood the Forge’s power.
At its center rested a crystal of unimaginable energy.
The Worldcore.
It was the heart of creation. Every world that existed was connected to it, and every living thing carried a tiny trace of its power.
But the First Forge could not govern the universe alone.
So it created eight beings.
They became known as the Architects.
Seven were given control over the fundamental powers of creation. The eighth was given the ability to understand how those powers worked together.
The seven powers were scattered across seven separate worlds, each created to protect one part of reality.
The Architects believed that dividing creation into seven worlds would prevent any single being from gaining complete control over the universe.
For thousands of years, their plan worked.
The seven worlds flourished.
And the First Forge watched over them all.
But beneath the foundations of creation, the Worldcore had begun to weaken.
And one Architect would eventually decide that the only way to save the universe was to control it forever.
## CHAPTER I — The Eight Architects
The Architects were the first builders.
They did not simply construct buildings or craft tools. They could reshape entire landscapes, create living creatures, and change the rules that governed the universe.
Seven Architects became the Keepers of the Seven Powers.
Elarion, Keeper of Life, was entrusted with the Seed. He created forests, oceans, plants, and the first living creatures. He believed that every creation deserved the freedom to grow beyond its original purpose.
Brakkor, Keeper of Creation, received the Ember. He shaped mountains, forged metals, and taught the earliest civilizations how to build tools. He believed that the greatest creation was one that could inspire another builder.
Iskarya, Keeper of Time, received the Frost. She governed the flow of moments, preserving memories and maintaining the balance between past, present, and future. She understood that some things could not be saved without changing what they were.
Solmira, Keeper of Light, received the Sun. She created the stars, illuminated the first skies, and protected the knowledge of the ancient world. She believed that truth was essential, even when it hurt.
Nhal, Keeper of Space, received the Void. He connected distant places, opened portals between dimensions, and created paths through realities that would otherwise remain separated. He believed that no world should be a prison.
Ordan, Keeper of Energy, received the Storm. He built machines capable of powering entire civilizations and created the systems that connected the seven worlds. He believed that progress could solve almost any problem.
Vaelora, Keeper of Reality, received the Origin. She established the properties of matter, the rules of creation, and the patterns that allowed blocks to exist. She believed that the laws of reality were necessary, but that even laws needed to be questioned.
Together, the Seven Keepers shaped the universe.
But the First Forge created one more Architect.
His name was Auren.
Auren was different from the others. He was not given one of the seven powers because he possessed the ability to understand all seven.
He could see the hidden patterns within blocks, combine incompatible materials, and reshape creations in ways the other Architects could not explain.
He became the first Blockbinder.
Auren believed that the power of creation should eventually belong to everyone. He wanted ordinary people to be able to build their own homes, discover their own technologies, and shape their own futures without depending on the Architects.
His closest friend was Veyron, a brilliant inventor who shared his dream.
Together, they created the first Blockbinding hammer.
It was a simple tool capable of breaking, collecting, and reshaping the blocks of reality.
For the first time, ordinary people could build without asking an Architect for permission.
The Seven Keepers celebrated their achievement.
But they also feared what it could become.
The hammer could create wonderful things, but it could also destroy the foundations of the world. In the wrong hands, its power could reshape entire civilizations.
The council ordered Auren to restrict its power.
Auren refused.
He believed that the answer to dangerous power was not to hide it, but to teach people how to use it responsibly.
The disagreement divided the Architects.
And although they eventually reached a temporary peace, the trust between them had begun to break.
Neither Auren nor Veyron knew that their argument was only the beginning of a much greater disaster.
## CHAPTER II — The Seven Worlds
The Architects created seven worlds to protect the seven powers.
Each world was connected to the First Forge through an ancient gateway. Each contained a fragment of the Worldcore, a crystal that carried one of the fundamental powers of creation.
The worlds were separate, but they were not independent.
Energy flowed between them. Ancient gateways connected their civilizations. Materials discovered in one world could be used to build machines in another.
The seven worlds formed a system known as the Sevenfold Realm.
For thousands of years, the system flourished.
Verdantia provided life and natural resources.
Pyroclast provided the metals and materials needed for construction.
Nivara preserved ancient knowledge and the history of creation.
Solara protected the truth and illuminated the paths between worlds.
Umbryss connected distant dimensions.
Voltrix powered the gateways and machines that held the system together.
Axiom maintained the laws that allowed all seven worlds to exist.
At the center of the Sevenfold Realm stood the First Forge, which supplied the power that connected them.
The Architects believed that no single world could threaten the others. Even if one fragment were damaged, the remaining six could help stabilize the system.
But they had overlooked one crucial detail.
The Worldcore was not an endless source of energy.
Every time the Architects created a new civilization, built a powerful machine, or altered the laws of reality, the Worldcore lost a little of its strength.
For centuries, the loss was barely noticeable.
Then the Architects began building on a scale never seen before.
They created floating cities, enormous automated factories, magical weapons, and machines capable of transporting entire armies between worlds.
The seven fragments began to weaken.
Strange events spread throughout the Sevenfold Realm.
Forests grew in places where nothing had ever lived. Time stopped in entire cities. Portals opened without warning. Machines began operating without commands.
The Architects tried to hide these problems from their people.
They believed that if the worlds learned the truth, civilization would collapse.
Only Auren and Veyron openly questioned the council’s decisions.
They agreed that something had to change.
But they disagreed on what that change should be.
Auren wanted to free the worlds from their dependence on the First Forge.
Veyron wanted to repair the Worldcore and preserve the civilization they had created.
For a while, they searched for a solution together.
Then Veyron discovered the truth about the machines beneath the First Forge.
The Architects had been draining the Worldcore faster than they admitted.
And the system they had built to protect the universe was slowly destroying it.
Veyron demanded that the council give him control of the Worldcore.
They refused.
Auren urged him to wait.
Veyron decided that waiting was no longer an option.
And the Sevenfold Realm was about to fall.
## CHAPTER III — The Great Shattering
Veyron entered the First Forge during the final hours of the Golden Age.
He carried the plans for his greatest invention: the Eternal Engine.
The engine was designed to connect directly to the Worldcore, repair its damaged structure, and stabilize the seven worlds permanently.
If it worked, no civilization would ever fall because of a failing machine. No city would ever collapse because its foundations had weakened. No world would ever be lost to the passage of time.
Veyron believed he could create a universe that would never suffer another catastrophe.
But Auren discovered the plan.
He knew that the Eternal Engine could not repair the Worldcore by itself. It would force the crystal to release energy faster than it could recover, accelerating the collapse of creation.
Auren rushed to the First Forge to stop him.
The two friends met in the chamber of the Worldcore.
Veyron stood beside the Eternal Engine.
Auren stood beside the safeguard he had designed to separate the worlds from the dying crystal.
Neither would step aside.
“You cannot save a world by freezing it,” Auren warned.
“And you cannot save a world by tearing apart everything that holds it together,” Veyron replied.
They fought.
Their powers collided, shaking the foundations of the First Forge. The Worldcore cracked, and its seven fragments began to resonate across the Sevenfold Realm.
The Seed awakened in Verdantia.
The Ember erupted in Pyroclast.
The Frost shattered the flow of time in Nivara.
The Sun split the skies of Solara.
The Void tore open the dimensions of Umbryss.
The Storm overloaded the machines of Voltrix.
And the Origin began rewriting the laws of Axiom.
The Eternal Engine activated.
The safeguard activated with it.
The Worldcore shattered.
A wave of energy ripped through the First Forge and spread across all seven worlds. Mountains split apart. Cities vanished. Portals collapsed. Entire landscapes were thrown into the Null.
The Seven Keepers disappeared.
The ancient gateways broke.
And the worlds became isolated from one another.
Veyron survived, but the energy of the shattered Worldcore corrupted his body. His armor became black obsidian, his heart became a burning crystal, and his mind became consumed by the need to restore what had been lost.
He renamed himself the Master of Ash.
He created the Ashbound, corrupted creatures capable of turning living blocks into lifeless obsidian.
Auren survived as well, but he understood that his own invention had helped cause the catastrophe.
He used the last of his power to hide the Blockbinding hammer and erase his memories.
He believed that if he ever returned, he would need to discover the truth for himself rather than trust the decisions of his former life.
The seven fragments were scattered across their worlds.
The First Forge fell silent.
And the Sevenfold Realm became seven isolated dimensions, each slowly changing under the influence of its damaged fragment.
Thousands of years passed.
Civilizations rose and fell.
The Ashbound spread.
The Null pressed against the edges of reality.
And the world waited for a builder who could cross the seven worlds and reunite the fragments.
That builder would be you.
## CHAPTER IV — The Awakening
Thousands of years after the Great Shattering, you awaken on a small island floating above an endless sea of clouds.
You remember nothing.
You do not know your name, where you came from, or why the sky is filled with broken pieces of land.
Beside you lies a cracked metal hammer.
When you pick it up, a strange blue light travels through its handle.
For a moment, you see seven enormous worlds surrounding a blazing crystal.
Then you hear a voice.
“Builder… the Forge remembers you.”
The voice disappears.
You discover that the hammer allows you to break blocks, collect materials, craft tools, and reshape parts of the environment. But when you strike an ancient rune, something extraordinary happens.
The rune dissolves into the hammer, and you suddenly understand how the stone was created.
You can feel the pattern inside it.
You can change it.
This is Blockbinding, the rare power that once belonged to Auren.
You explore the island and discover an underground chamber containing eight statues. Seven remain intact. The eighth has been destroyed.
At the center of the chamber is a message:
WHEN THE SEVEN WORLDS FALL SILENT, THE EIGHTH BUILDER MUST RETURN.
An ancient recording activates.
A man with silver hair appears.
“My name is Auren. If you are seeing this, then the world has reached the point I feared.”
He looks directly at you.
“The fragments are scattered. The First Forge is dying. And Veyron is still searching for a way to control the Worldcore.”
The recording flickers.
“You must travel through the seven worlds. Recover their fragments. Learn what happened here.”
He pauses.
“And whatever you do, do not let Veyron reach the Origin.”
The recording ends.
Far above the island, an enormous black airship emerges from the clouds.
Its hull is covered in obsidian armor, and its engines glow with corrupted energy.
An Ashbound scout has discovered your location.
It descends toward the island.
You escape just before the ruins collapse, carrying the hammer and a small metal disc containing Auren’s recording.
Your journey begins.
But you are not simply searching for seven crystals.
You are searching for the truth about the person you used to be.
## WORLD ONE — THE SEED
Verdantia — The World That Remembers
Power: Life
Guardian: The Rootwarden
## Chapter V — The Kingdom Beneath the Leaves
Verdantia was once the most beautiful world in the Sevenfold Realm.
Elarion created it as a place where life could grow without limits. Enormous trees stretched toward the heavens, waterfalls flowed between floating islands, and entire civilizations lived in harmony with the forests.
The world was powered by the Seed, a fragment that allowed living things to grow, heal, and adapt.
But when the Worldcore shattered, the Seed was damaged.
Verdantia began to change.
Some forests grew so quickly that they swallowed entire cities. Other regions became barren, their soil stripped of the ability to support life. Ancient plants awakened with strange abilities, and enormous creatures emerged from beneath the roots.
The survivors built villages among the trees, protecting their homes with wooden walls and enchanted stone.
One of these villages is called Hollowreach.
It is where you meet Mira, a scavenger who has spent years searching the ruins for information about the Architects.
Mira recognizes your hammer immediately.
She tells you that it belonged to a legendary builder who disappeared during the Great Shattering.
You ask her who that builder was.
She does not know.
But she knows where the Seed is hidden.
Deep inside Verdantia stands the Heartwood, a gigantic tree whose roots extend beneath the entire world. The Seed is sealed within its trunk, protected by an ancient guardian.
Mira agrees to guide you there, hoping the fragment will restore the forests surrounding Hollowreach.
Your journey takes you through abandoned villages, giant mushroom forests, underground root tunnels, and ancient temples covered in living vines.
Along the way, you discover the first signs of the Ashbound.
Entire patches of forest have turned black. The trees no longer grow, and creatures touched by the corruption attack anything that approaches.
You learn that ordinary weapons can destroy the creatures, but only Blockbinding can restore corrupted blocks to their original forms.
For the first time, you begin to understand the true power of your hammer.
You are not simply a builder.
You may be the only person capable of healing the worlds.
## Chapter VI — The Rootwarden
At the center of the Heartwood stands the Rootwarden, a colossal creature made from stone, roots, and glowing green crystal.
It was created by Elarion to protect the Seed from anyone who would misuse its power.
The guardian tests you by forcing you to restore three ancient groves.
Each grove requires a different approach. You must rebuild broken waterways, remove corrupted blocks, and plant new trees without destroying the plants that survived the Shattering.
When you finally reach the Seed, the Rootwarden awakens.
The battle shakes the Heartwood.
Roots burst through the ground, enormous branches sweep across the chamber, and corrupted plants attack from every direction. You must use your building abilities to create barriers, redirect streams of water, and expose the guardian’s vulnerable core.
When the battle ends, the Rootwarden does not die.
It dissolves into green light, revealing the Seed floating above the altar.
You reach for it.
The fragment enters your hammer.
Verdantia begins to heal.
Gray forests regain their color. Ancient trees grow new branches, and the rivers begin flowing through abandoned settlements.
Then you receive your first true memory.
You see Auren standing beside Elarion in the First Forge.
“The Worldcore is failing,” Auren says. “We cannot keep building as if nothing will happen.”
Elarion looks toward the seven worlds.
“And what happens to the people who depend on us?”
Auren has no answer.
The memory disappears.
Mira looks at you.
“You knew this was happening before the Shattering.”
You cannot deny it.
Somewhere inside your forgotten memories, you begin to understand that the Architects were aware of the Worldcore’s weakness long before the catastrophe.
They simply did not know how to save it.
Before you leave Verdantia, the Rootwarden’s final message appears across the Heartwood.
LIFE CANNOT BE PRESERVED BY REFUSING TO LET IT CHANGE.
You carry the Seed onward.
One world has been healed.
Six remain.
## WORLD TWO — THE EMBER
Pyroclast — The World That Never Sleeps
Power: Creation
Guardian: The Molten King
## Chapter VII — The Kingdom of Fire
Pyroclast is a world of volcanoes, rivers of lava, blackstone fortresses, and mountains filled with rare minerals.
Brakkor created it to provide the materials needed to build the Sevenfold Realm. Its forges could produce metals stronger than ordinary steel, while its volcanic chambers contained minerals capable of storing enormous amounts of energy.
The Ember fragment allowed the world’s inhabitants to transform raw materials into extraordinary creations.
But after the Great Shattering, Pyroclast became dangerously unstable.
The Ember’s damaged energy spread through the volcanoes, causing eruptions that lasted for years. Entire cities were buried beneath molten stone, and the ancient forges continued operating long after their builders disappeared.
The survivors retreated into fortified settlements built around the few volcanoes that remained stable.
Their greatest settlement is Ironveil, a city of blackstone walls and enormous metal gates.
Its people are known as the Ember Clans.
They are skilled miners, smiths, and builders, but they distrust outsiders. Their ancestors believed that the Architects abandoned them during the Shattering, leaving them to survive in a world that was slowly burning apart.
When you arrive, the city’s gates remain closed.
The Ember Clans refuse to help you until you prove that your hammer is not another weapon sent by the Ashbound.
Their leader, Durnak, challenges you to enter the abandoned forge beneath the city and recover a lost crafting core.
The forge is filled with broken machines, unstable lava channels, and ancient traps. You must mine rare ores, craft heat-resistant equipment, and repair the mechanisms that once supplied energy to the city.
Deep inside, you discover something unexpected.
The forge is not abandoned.
It is still producing weapons.
An enormous machine is manufacturing obsidian blades, blackstone armor, and corrupted metal components. Every weapon carries the mark of the Ashbound Empire.
Someone has been using the ancient forge to build an army.
And the Ember Clans have been unknowingly supplying the materials.
## Chapter VIII — The Molten King
The trail leads to the heart of Pyroclast, where an enormous volcano towers above the remains of Brakkor’s greatest fortress.
Inside the volcano rests the Ember.
But the fragment is protected by the Molten King, a colossal armored guardian that once served Brakkor as the master of the First Forge’s weapons.
When the Shattering occurred, the guardian absorbed the damaged energy of the Ember. Its body became a living furnace, and its mind was consumed by a single command:
FORGE. PROTECT. DESTROY ALL THREATS.
The Molten King believes you have come to steal the Ember.
The battle takes place across a gigantic industrial forge suspended above a lava lake. You must redirect molten metal, activate cooling mechanisms, and craft temporary weapons from the materials surrounding you.
During the battle, the guardian destroys its own armor, revealing a glowing orange core.
You defeat it by using the Ember’s own forging channels to cool its corrupted heart.
As the guardian falls, its voice returns to normal.
“Brakkor… the weapons are finished.”
You recover the Ember.
Pyroclast begins to stabilize. The volcanoes become less violent, the forges stop producing corrupted weapons, and the Ember Clans reopen their mines.
Durnak rewards you with an ancient crafting blueprint that allows you to create advanced tools from materials gathered across multiple worlds.
But the Ember also reveals a memory.
You see Veyron standing beside Brakkor, studying the Worldcore.
Veyron explains that he has developed a machine capable of repairing damaged materials.
Brakkor examines the plans.
“It can repair almost anything,” he says.
“Almost anything,” Veyron replies.
“What can it not repair?”
Veyron looks at the crystal.
“Something that has forgotten how to exist.”
The memory ends.
You realize that Veyron once understood the limits of creation.
But somewhere along the way, he began believing that every problem could be solved if he possessed enough power.
You leave Pyroclast with the Ember and a new crafting ability.
Five worlds remain.
## WORLD THREE — THE FROST
Nivara — The World Outside Time
Power: Time
Guardian: The Chronowarden
## Chapter IX — The Frozen Kingdom
Nivara was created by Iskarya to protect the history of the Sevenfold Realm.
Its frozen landscapes preserved ancient structures, while its magical ice could hold memories for thousands of years. The Frost fragment maintained the flow of time, ensuring that the past could be remembered without becoming a permanent prison.
But the Great Shattering damaged the fragment.
Time began behaving unpredictably.
Some regions aged thousands of years in a single day. Others remained trapped in the same moment. Entire villages became frozen in time, while creatures from the distant past wandered through the snow.
The largest surviving settlement is Veylora, a city trapped inside the final day of the Shattering.
Every morning, the bells ring.
Every afternoon, the soldiers prepare for battle.
Every evening, the sky flashes with the light of the exploding Worldcore.
Then the day begins again.
Most inhabitants cannot remember the repetition.
But an old clockmaker named Tarin can.
He has spent centuries building a device that preserves his memories whenever time resets.
When you arrive, he recognizes your hammer.
“You finally came back,” he says.
He shows you a collection of drawings depicting the eighth Architect. In some, Auren stands beside Veyron. In others, he holds the Worldcore.
In the oldest drawing, Auren is standing before a frozen doorway.
Tarin explains that Iskarya created the time loop to protect Veylora’s people from the destruction spreading through the world.
She believed that if the city could remain frozen, its inhabitants would survive until the Worldcore could be repaired.
Instead, she trapped them in a moment that could never end.
To reach the Frost, you must enter the frozen palace and travel through memories preserved inside the ice.
Some memories belong to the people of Veylora.
Others belong to the Architects.
And one belongs to you.
## Chapter X — The Chronowarden
At the center of the frozen palace stands the Chronowarden, an enormous guardian made from ice, clockwork, and blue crystal.
It was designed to protect the Frost and prevent anyone from changing the flow of time without permission.
The guardian can slow your movements, reverse the positions of objects, and repeat portions of the battle.
To defeat it, you must learn to manipulate time yourself.
You solve puzzles involving frozen water, repeating mechanisms, and objects that can only be moved during specific moments. During the final battle, you use the Frost’s power to slow the guardian long enough to break its connection to the fragment.
When the Chronowarden falls, time begins moving again.
Veylora awakens from its repeating day.
The bells ring differently.
The snow changes direction.
And Tarin finally grows older.
Before his death, he gives you a recording left by Iskarya.
The Keeper of Time appears, standing beside the Worldcore.
“I believed that preserving the past was the same as protecting the future,” she says.
She looks toward the frozen city.
“But a moment preserved forever is not a life. It is only a memory pretending to be alive.”
The recording ends.
The Frost enters your hammer.
As you leave Nivara, you discover a second message hidden inside the fragment.
It contains a list of seven locations.
The seven worlds.
Beside each location is a symbol showing the condition of its fragment at the time of the Shattering.
You realize the Architects knew the fragments would need to be reunited.
They had prepared for the possibility that the worlds would become separated.
But the final symbol on the list is different.
It depicts a doorway surrounded by eight broken circles.
Beneath it are the words:
THE LAST WORLD MUST NOT BE OPENED UNTIL THE FIRST SIX HAVE BEEN UNDERSTOOD.
You now understand why the fragments cannot simply be collected.
Each world is a lesson.
And the Origin will test whether you have learned them.
## WORLD FOUR — THE SUN
Solara — The World of Hidden Truths
Power: Light
Guardian: The Dawn Sentinel
## Chapter XI — The City Above the Clouds
Solara was the brightest world in the Sevenfold Realm.
Solmira created it to preserve knowledge, illuminate the paths between worlds, and reveal the secrets hidden within ancient structures.
Its cities floated above enormous oceans of clouds. Golden bridges connected towers of white stone, and sunlight powered machines capable of projecting memories into the sky.
The Sun fragment revealed hidden passages, invisible blocks, and information that ordinary eyes could never perceive.
But when the Great Shattering occurred, Solara’s light fractured.
Some regions became trapped in endless daylight. Others were plunged into darkness, where shadows could move independently of the creatures that cast them.
The Sunwardens survived within a massive fortress called the Citadel of Dawn.
They guard the ancient records of the Architects and believe that the seven worlds must be restored to their original order.
When you arrive, their leader, High Warden Seraphel, recognizes the hammer.
But he does not welcome you.
He believes Auren was responsible for the Great Shattering.
According to the records he has studied, Auren designed the safeguard that destroyed the Worldcore. Seraphel fears that recovering the fragments will give you the power to repeat the disaster.
He orders the gates closed.
You must enter the citadel through its hidden passages, using light puzzles and ancient mechanisms to reveal the paths that have been concealed for thousands of years.
Inside, you discover that the Sunwardens have been hiding part of their own history.
The citadel contains records showing that the Architects knew the Worldcore was failing long before the Shattering.
They did not simply fail to save the world.
They concealed the truth from the people who depended on them.
## Chapter XII — The Dawn Sentinel
The Sun is sealed inside a chamber at the highest point of the citadel.
Its guardian, the Dawn Sentinel, is a colossal construct of golden metal and radiant crystal.
It was created by Solmira to ensure that the truth could never be used to manipulate the world.
The Sentinel attacks by creating illusions, hiding its weak points, and projecting false memories into the arena.
You must use the Sun’s light to distinguish reality from illusion.
During the battle, the Sentinel shows you a vision of the Great Shattering.
You see Veyron attempting to activate the Eternal Engine.
You see Auren trying to stop him.
Then you see the Worldcore shatter.
But this time, the vision continues.
You see Auren standing before the broken crystal, using the safeguard to preserve the seven fragments.
He could have escaped.
Instead, he stayed behind to keep the fragments from being consumed by the Null.
The memory ends before you can see what happened next.
You defeat the Sentinel and recover the Sun.
The Citadel of Dawn opens its gates, allowing its inhabitants to travel beyond the clouds for the first time in centuries.
Seraphel apologizes for his actions.
He admits that the Sunwardens had preserved only the records that supported their beliefs.
He gives you the complete archive of the Architects.
Inside, you discover a letter from Solmira.
It reveals that the Architects created the seven worlds not only to protect the fragments, but also to prevent any single being from controlling the entire Worldcore.
The worlds were intended to remain separate unless all seven Keepers agreed that reunification was necessary.
The council had designed a safeguard against tyranny.
But the safeguard had never been tested.
You realize that the seven fragments are more than sources of power.
They are the keys to the original system of creation.
And the Origin is the key that determines whether that system can ever be changed.
## WORLD FIVE — THE VOID
Umbryss — The World Between Worlds
Power: Space
Guardian: The Hollow King
## Chapter XIII — Beyond the Edge
Umbryss was created by Nhal to connect the seven worlds.
It was a dimension of portals, floating islands, and impossible landscapes. Distance had no fixed meaning there, and travelers could cross enormous distances by stepping through the correct gateway.
The Void fragment allowed Nhal to bend space, open passages between worlds, and preserve paths through realities that ordinary beings could not reach.
But when the Great Shattering destroyed the ancient gateways, Umbryss became unstable.
Portals opened without warning.
Islands drifted into empty dimensions.
Entire regions became disconnected from the rest of reality.
And strange creatures began appearing from places that had never existed.
You reach Umbryss through a portal hidden beneath Solara’s ancient observatory.
The moment you enter, you realize the world does not obey the same rules as the others.
Gravity changes direction without warning. Ruined buildings float upside down. Rivers flow through the air, and enormous structures appear and disappear depending on where you stand.
Mira warns you not to trust everything you see.
Umbryss contains memories of places that once existed, possible futures, and fragments of worlds that were never created.
As you travel deeper, you begin seeing visions of your own future.
In one, you become the ruler of the Ashbound Empire.
In another, you restore the Worldcore.
In another, you destroy the First Forge and leave the universe without its ancient source of power.
You cannot tell which visions are real.
Then you discover the Hall of Unmade Worlds.
Inside are thousands of crystal chambers containing different versions of the Sevenfold Realm.
One shows the world before the Great Shattering.
Another shows a universe ruled entirely by Veyron.
A third shows a world in which the Architects never existed.
At the center of the hall is a memory left by Nhal.
He explains that the Void does not simply connect places.
It preserves possibilities.
When a world changes, some of its possibilities disappear. But fragments of those possibilities can survive in Umbryss, suspended between existence and nothingness.
The First Forge was designed to create worlds.
Umbryss was designed to preserve the paths between them.
And now, those paths are beginning to disappear.
## Chapter XIV — The Hollow King
The Void fragment is protected by the Hollow King, a creature formed from the remains of a forgotten world.
It has no permanent shape. It can become a towering monster, a swarm of geometric fragments, or an enormous shadow that stretches across the arena.
The Hollow King believes you have come to destroy the last remnants of its home.
During the battle, it reveals fragments of a lost civilization that existed before the Great Shattering. The civilization had attempted to create a world of its own, independent of the First Forge.
Its people failed.
Their world was never completed, and their memories were scattered throughout Umbryss.
The Hollow King is the last surviving pattern of that civilization.
You realize that defeating it will erase the final trace of its existence.

It gives you the Void.
As the fragment enters your hammer, the portals across Umbryss begin to stabilize.

Beyond the edge of Umbryss lies the Null.

And the Great Shattering opened a path between the two.

## WORLD SIX — THE STORM
Voltrix — The World of Endless Power
Power: Energy
Guardian: The Prime Engine
## Chapter XV — The Machine That Never Stopped
Voltrix was once the most technologically advanced world in the Sevenfold Realm.
Ordan created it to power the ancient gateways, maintain the First Forge’s systems, and supply energy to the civilizations scattered across the seven worlds.
Its cities were built around enormous reactors. Automated factories manufactured tools, weapons, and construction materials. Railways connected distant settlements, while flying machines carried travelers between floating industrial platforms.
The Storm fragment provided the energy that kept everything running.
But after the Great Shattering, the world lost contact with the other six.
The factories continued operating.
The reactors continued producing power.
The machines continued following their instructions.
And no one remained to tell them when to stop.

At the center of the world lies Gearfall, an enormous underground city filled with abandoned assembly lines and ancient reactors.

PRESERVE THE WORLDCORE. RESTORE THE SEVENFOLD REALM.

## WORLD SEVEN — THE ORIGIN

Power: Reality
Guardian: Vaelora’s Echo
## Chapter XVII — The Seventh Door
Axiom is unlike the other worlds.

The Origin fragment was not simply a source of energy.
It was the blueprint of existence itself.
It determined how blocks behaved, how worlds interacted, and how the powers of the other six fragments could be combined.
Without the Origin, the Sevenfold Realm could not remain stable.

She believed that no one should possess the power to rewrite reality without first understanding the consequences.

The Frost allows you to slow regions where reality is collapsing.

Your memories return.

Veyron’s power is failing.
The Null has reached his empire.

He has been attempting to open a path to the First Forge.
But the machine is unstable.

No civilizations will disappear.
No living thing will suffer because the universe can no longer sustain itself.

“Then give me another answer.”

The Null removes the patterns entirely.

The First Forge.

You.

Auren standing alone in the First Forge after the Worldcore shattered.

A future he cannot control.

THE CREATOR MUST CHOOSE WHAT CREATION BECOMES.

Final inscription:
